1. Background & Abstract

Ghana’s 261 districts exhibit pronounced geographic inequity in health system performance. This study applies a composite performance index integrating facility density, service coverage, workforce ratios, geographic accessibility, quality indicators, and health equity metrics. Spatial autocorrelation (Moran’s I = 0.62) confirms strong geographic clustering, with high-burden districts concentrated in the Northern, Savannah, and Upper regions. Machine learning (XGBoost, AUC = 0.91) identifies poverty rate as the dominant driver (28.4% SHAP contribution). Findings guide targeted resource allocation to close the 47-point North–South performance gap.

2. Data Sources

DHIMS2 — District Health Information Management System (facility census, service utilisation, workforce data, 2020–2023). NHIS — National Health Insurance Authority (coverage and claims data). GSS — Ghana Statistical Service (2021 PHC population, poverty data). GHS — Ghana Health Service (quality indicators). GADM v4.1 — Administrative boundaries (261 districts, 16 regions).

3. Ethical Statement

This study uses aggregated administrative and national survey data. No individual-level or identifiable data were used. Data were obtained from publicly available or open-access national repositories. Ethical review was not required under Ghana Health Service guidelines for aggregate spatial analyses.

8. Discussion & Conclusion

Ghana’s health system performance is strongly patterned spatially, with a persistent North–South divide driven primarily by poverty and geographic inaccessibility. The 47 HH cluster districts require prioritised resource allocation under Ghana’s National Health Policy. The XGBoost model (AUC 0.91) provides a replicable targeting tool for district-level health system strengthening. Future work should integrate longitudinal DHIMS2 data to assess trajectory and intervention impact.
